# Curating and validating a photographic FLUX LoRA

Research and decision framework for Nanouk LoRA · 25 November 2026

## Decision summary

- Start with close portraits and waist-up lifestyle. Require recognizable likeness in both framings, with separate checks for straight-on, left and right three-quarter views, and the expressions the creator actually publishes. Judge the face both at native crop scale and in the composed post. Activate full-body fashion, strict profile and extreme angles only if the brief calls for them. [Meta-LoRA](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22352) · [DreamBench++](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/71ad539a57b1fd49b19e5c80070cb8b9-Abstract-Conference.html)
- Count distinct usable capture groups and coverage after training preprocessing. Several crops from the same source can expose different pixels to the trainer, but they share the same facial evidence and add training weight. Keep variants together in one split. A new session that breaks an expression–outfit–background correlation can contribute more than another burst from the dominant setup. The benefit for this project is a pilot hypothesis. [Meta-LoRA, input augmentation](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22352) · [sd-scripts dataset configuration](https://github.com/kohya-ss/sd-scripts/blob/main/docs/config_README-en.md)
- Use 12–24 distinct training groups as a starting budget for these two briefs if the required combinations and references are present. Treat 3–10 as a possible feasibility probe, especially when testing fit or wiring. Try 24–36 only if the added groups bring useful coverage or a new brief needs it. Each range is a project heuristic, excludes held-out references, and can change after the pilot. Published work establishes feasibility in specific settings, with limited evidence for the size or framing balance a real creator needs. [DreamBooth](https://dreambooth.github.io/) · [FLUX.1 [dev] DreamBooth LoRA synthetic-person experiment, §S3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26627v1)
- Match base and LoRA comparisons, then test a small, targeted replacement or addition after inspecting captions, preprocessing, training checkpoints and applied strength. Judge each required scenario by likeness, prompt adherence, creator approval and yield within a fixed budget. Train and evaluate locally with consent and agreed use of the captures.

Assumptions used below are provisional. No post briefs, installed versions, checkpoint, quantization, GPU, generation budget or publishing arrangement were specified. The research uses public evidence and fictional examples. The archive was left untouched. The candidate below is vanilla FLUX.1 [dev] for a rights-cleared pilot. A local smoke test will confirm the installed pairing.

## Facial evidence and what to keep

Set priorities against the briefs. The side convention for this project uses anatomical sides. `left_3q` exposes more of the creator's left cheek, and `right_3q` exposes more of the right. Record sides on an orientation-corrected, unmirrored image. Broad yaw bins for reviewers can start at <15° straight-on, 15–65° three-quarter, and >65° strongly turned/profile. Note whether the strict profile silhouette is visible and reserve that subset for a profile requirement. Use these bins as approximate curation labels.

| Attribute | Keep and prioritize | Conditional use or exclude | Basis and confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Straight-on and both three-quarter views | Get a neutral straight-on portrait for eye/brow spacing, nose and jaw relationships. Each three-quarter side adds visible cheek, nose projection and jaw contour, including natural asymmetries. Prioritize seeing the natural smile in at least one close three-quarter setup and a matching waist-up post. | Keep extra favorite-angle imagery if it adds a useful expression, lighting condition or framing. Strict profile and extreme head turns need side-appropriate references if published. They are otherwise optional. | **Transferable + heuristic**, moderate. Actual creator priorities and side-specific test failures could shift the mix. [CFP](https://engineering.jhu.edu/vpatel36/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/WACV2016_CFP.pdf) |
| Camera elevation, head pitch, gaze | Anchor at roughly eye level with level head, then capture the common elevated or lower camera angle if the brief uses it. Vary direct and off-camera gaze. Label camera elevation independently of chin pitch since a high camera and lowered chin do different things to eyelids and jaw visibility. | Use an extreme low angle or strongly tucked chin when that look is intentional and has a reference. Avoid training solely from overhead selfies with the chin tucked. | **Imaging transfer + heuristic**, moderate. Add the actual combination if repeatable failures track it. [Perspective study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4760932/) |
| Expression | Keep neutral, relaxed smile and natural teeth-showing smile if used in posts. Compare how lips, teeth line, cheeks and eyes move. Get the smile at the intended camera distance, especially waist-up. | A deliberate broad grin or squint earns a slot if the creator needs it. Exclude a blink, motion-smeared mouth, or expression that misrepresents the approved look unless a separate brief needs it. | **Heuristic**, moderate. The relative image counts per expression are unknown. |
| Camera distance, orientation and mirror | Prefer images whose proportions match independent references. Record camera-to-face distance where known and working perspective separately from framing. Use an unmirrored original where possible. Use a known asymmetry or capture record to confirm mirror state and document any correction. | A near-camera selfie with an exaggerated nose may be useful for an intentional selfie look. Quarantine unresolved mirror status for side-specific coverage and disable automatic flip for v1. Label vertical, horizontal or square without rotating otherwise valid composition. | **Imaging transfer + heuristic**, high for distance effects, moderate for the mirror/flip policy. [Ward et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5876805/) · [sd-scripts options](https://github.com/kohya-ss/sd-scripts/blob/main/docs/config_README-en.md) |
| Lighting and exposure | Use soft neutral light as a stable face anchor and a meaningful directional or outdoor variation if used in posts. Check both eyes, cheek edges, nose and lip separation, plus clipped highlight/shadow. | Hard side light, backlight and colored casts can teach intended looks. Count them as face anchors only when the needed features remain visible. Exclude irrecoverable facial clipping or shadows for the relevant scenario. | **Transferable + heuristic**, moderate. Pilot against the expected exposure and contrast. [NIST FATE](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.IR.8485) |
| Editing, makeup, hair, accessories and occlusion | Prefer originals or mild tonal corrections. Compare against the creator's approved references to distinguish stable features, cosmetics or hairstyle she wants preserved, and attributes she wants to control. Keep visible real freckles, asymmetrical brows or smile if part of her chosen likeness. Label changes in makeup and styling. | Keep one intended glasses or hair-over-face example if useful, with unobstructed evidence for the same view. Exclude obvious face-reshaping filters, smoothed-away landmarks, AI restoration that redraws features, and unknown severe retouching. Record the reason. | **Heuristic**, moderate. Let creator judgments resolve the desired baseline and style variants. |
| Framing, body and scene | Close and head-and-shoulders photos expose facial detail. Waist-up images teach pose, arms, silhouette, garment drape and face visibility at intended scale. Record relevant poses, body proportions, outfits and backgrounds. Include vertical framing if the delivery is vertical. | A usable full-body frame can support an outfit or body brief even with a tiny face. Treat it as context evidence unless it meets the face-specific check at training scale. Check edge stretching, long coats concealing silhouette and awkward crop boundaries. | **Heuristic + adjacent research**, moderate. Add full-body coverage if real posts call for it. [Pro-Pose, different task and conditioning](https://pro-pose-portrait.github.io/) |

Treat alignment with a stable feature as a comparison across independent photographs. A larger nose that appears only in arm's-length photos is evidence for a projection effect. An asymmetrical smile visible in two setups at comparable angle is evidence for a feature to preserve if approved by the creator. Cosmetics and intentional retouching can be part of an editorial look. Represent them alongside suitable less-altered evidence when the team wants prompt control.

### Inspect actual training pixels

1. Keep local originals and work on staged copies. Normalize EXIF orientation, check for clipping and mirror corrections, and use unique basenames for image/sidecar pairs. FluxGym's inspected `resize_image` opens the stored image and scales its **short edge** to the supplied size using Pillow Lanczos, before writing the staged image. It can upscale. The stock UI showed a resize value of 512 when reviewed. A 2400×3600 file at 768 becomes 768×1152 at this first stage. [FluxGym resize, dataset staging and UI code](https://github.com/cocktailpeanut/fluxgym/blob/main/app.py)
2. Read the emitted launch command and `dataset.toml`. The inspected FluxGym TOML generator sets resolution and batch size and does not explicitly enable aspect-ratio buckets. The reviewed upstream sd-scripts code uses a single target resolution when aspect bucketing is disabled. If supported by the installed version, explicitly set and verify `enable_bucket=true` for varied ratios and record `bucket_reso_steps`, bucket policy, and crop behavior. `bucket_no_upscale` applies to the trainer's input, after any FluxGym upscale. When enabled in reviewed upstream code, it derives buckets from image sizes and ignores min/max bucket bounds. If the shipped trainer exposes `--debug_dataset`, run/preview it locally on staged images and compare actual logged bucket sizes and frames. Otherwise reproduce the installed resize/crop path locally and spot-check the outputs and logs. Do this before classifying coverage. [Generated TOML](https://github.com/cocktailpeanut/fluxgym/blob/main/app.py) · [sd-scripts configuration](https://github.com/kohya-ss/sd-scripts/blob/main/docs/config_README-en.md) · [Bucket selection and preprocessing](https://github.com/kohya-ss/sd-scripts/blob/main/library/dataset.py) · [Trainer](https://github.com/kohya-ss/sd-scripts/blob/main/train_network.py)
3. At 100% on the final training frame, record the face box width and height in pixels. Use an axis-aligned box around the visible anatomical face, brow/forehead to chin, cheek to cheek or the visible profile contour. Exclude hair volume and neck. Note cutoffs explicitly. Check eyelid/brow boundaries, nose contour, lips and teeth when visible, and jaw at the relevant view. Inspect motion blur, oversharpening, compression, clipping and distortion. Compare the source at 100% if the processed image appears artificially sharp. A large upscaled face can still have low detail.
4. At a 768 or 1024 nominal training setting, provisionally use at least ~160 px face height plus clear relevant features to flag an anchor candidate. At ~96–159 px ask whether it is sufficient for the exact brief, especially waist-up, and mark `marginal` or `clear` after viewing. Below ~96 px, default to context alone unless reviewed evidence and a pilot justify credit. These are review queues, with no biometric or LoRA cutoff implied. A 140 px sharp waist-up face might qualify after explicit review. A 300 px blurred face receives no face credit. A cropped forehead in a valid post can count if all its essential cues remain visible.
5. A crop of a sharp wider photo can improve signal in a training bucket and remove irrelevant background. It cannot add independent detail or recover blurred or deleted features. Prefer swapping in a crop if the wide original's pose/body evidence is already represented. If both are trained as an explicit treatment, record both exposures, keep their IDs in one capture group and hold the exposure budget constant if feasible. The group still earns one representative vote in readiness. **Direct FLUX + heuristic**, moderate for crop feasibility, low for the best ratio here. [Meta-LoRA augmentation](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22352)

A controlled framing test could use 20 equally repeated independent groups, with 14 close and six wider in the face-heavy set versus 10 and 10 in a matched mixed set. These are proposed allocations. Use the same core face/combination coverage, replace four comparable groups across arms, match setups, quality and caption policy where possible, and keep the effective update budget and inference grid fixed. Report changes by post type for face scores, body realism and yield. If expression or lighting also changed because the archive could only supply certain wider groups, record the confound.

## Dataset readiness

### Count and split

- A **capture group** is one independent photographic observation for audit purposes. Put byte duplicates, crops, retouches, mirrored exports, video frames of the same moment, and near-identical burst frames in one group. Group near-duplicates across folders using sequence/time context and a local contact sheet. Merge ambiguous copies conservatively. A new shutter press earns its own group when it gives observably different useful evidence, such as a deliberate change of yaw, expression, camera position or pose. A small blink or a different crop earns no extra group. Assign one training representative per group for v1. Log any intentional extra training variant as an extra exposure, and keep group IDs stable across dataset versions.
- A **setup** is a repeatable shooting arrangement within a session. Use a new setup ID when camera distance/placement, lighting, outfit/background, or shooting arrangement changes materially. A session is one continuous capture event. A small pose or expression change can produce a new group within the same setup. For readiness, aim for two or more training sessions and cap each required scenario's training credit at two groups per setup. A deliberate burst with nearly unchanged evidence remains one group before this cap.
- Reserve authentic evaluation photos early. Prefer an entire held-out session, or physically distinct capture setups if only one session is available. Record the resulting independence limit. Across train and holdout there must be zero group overlap and zero setup overlap. Keep videos, alternate crops/edits and near-duplicates within the same split. Keep references out of training, training sample inputs, detailer references and inference conditioning. Save two distinct, clear reference groups per required scenario, including a matching view, expression and framing. Aim for different held-out setups and add another reference for genuine variation if a scenario cannot be judged confidently. A single held-out setup can supply two distinct groups, but note correlated lighting/perspective and plan a second setup before any ambiguous result is accepted.
- Label the dimensions in the audit CSV and cross-tab the ones important to the briefs. Start with expression × outfit/background, yaw × light, expression × framing, and yaw × camera elevation/pitch. Check eyewear, hair, gaze, perspective and orientation when coupled to one required look. For example, add a smile in a second outfit and a neutral photo in the smile outfit if smiles always share one wardrobe/setting. Keep some same-look near/far pairs captured independently when they isolate framing. Avoid a full factorial shoot.

### Priority combinations and coverage levels

These defaults assume vertical 4:5 delivery and photographic lighting. Rank the scenarios with the creator before auditing. A group qualifies only if its representative meets the scenario definition, has `face_detail=clear` and usable source quality, and preserves the necessary cues after preprocessing. A face partly obscured by an intentional accessory can qualify only if that scenario expressly calls for it and the reviewer can still judge the required cues. A group may qualify for multiple coherent scenario definitions, but count it once within each scenario. Holdout photos use the same visual criteria at the review framing and do not enter the training count.

| ID | Post type and role | Required facial combination | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | Close portrait, routine | Close or head-and-shoulders, frontal, neutral, eye-level with near-level pitch, readable soft light | 3 |
| R2 | Close portrait, routine with lateral likeness check | Close or head-and-shoulders, left three-quarter, relaxed smile, readable cheek and jaw | 2 |
| R3 | Waist-up lifestyle, priority stress case | Waist-up, right three-quarter, teeth smile, visible eyes, mouth and jaw in a believable lifestyle setting | 3 |
| R4 | Waist-up lifestyle, routine | Waist-up, left three-quarter, relaxed smile, clear face and plausible seated or standing pose | 2 |
| C1 | Full-body fashion, conditional | Head-to-toe with faithful silhouette and scale-appropriate face, when commissioned | Assign when activated |
| C2 | Profile-heavy work, conditional | Specified side and true profile, with readable expected silhouette and target framing | Assign when activated |

Record camera elevation, head pitch, light and composition even when a scenario leaves them open. Add a directional-light or high-camera requirement if the planned posts need it and the creator supplies independent references. Optional cases are reported without entering a required aggregate.

| Coverage level | Training evidence per required scenario |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Zero confirmed face-qualified groups. |
| 1 | At least one confirmed group from one setup. A whole session's repeated variations in the same setup stay at level 1. |
| 2 | Two or more confirmed groups spanning at least two training setups, with fewer than four groups after the cap of two per setup. |
| 3 | Four or more confirmed groups spanning at least two training setups after the cap. Extra groups beyond this point earn zero extra scenario credit. |

Use the following editable **heuristic**. Moderate confidence for the ordering of priorities, low for exact weights and cutoffs. Review them when post mix or compute/edit budget changes.

| Component | Points and inputs |
| --- | --- |
| Source quality (`Q`) | 30 × sum of representative `source_quality` scores / (2 × N), where N is the number of proposed training representatives. Score 2 for usable clean pixels, 1 for a documented compromise with useful evidence, 0 for an unresolved or failed item. Use the postprocessed training input when looking for irreversible source defects and check the original as needed. Q = 0 when N = 0. Never train a quality-0 item. |
| Diversity (`D`) | 25 × sum of the five diversity ratings / 10. Score 0, 1 or 2 in each row below. Calculate on quality-verified actual training representatives. Use stable setup IDs and count each group once per rating. |
| Required combination coverage (`C`) | 45 × sum of (scenario weight × level) / (3 × sum of active required weights). Exclude optional cases. A failed scenario remains failed even when the total is high. |
| Total | `Q + D + C`, out of 100. Keep underlying counts, the five ratings and the scenario checklist with the dataset manifest. |

| Diversity rating | 1 point | 2 points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Session/setup | At least two usable training setups. | At least three distinct usable setups across two or more sessions. |
| Light | Two usable lighting direction/contrast categories relevant to the briefs, observed across two setups. | Those two categories each appear in at least two setups. |
| Styling and scene | At least two relevant looks across outfits, settings, hair or makeup, in different setups. | At least two outfit looks and two background types in different setups with at least one outfit or background reused in a different combination. |
| Camera/framing/body | Both target framings have usable evidence. | Both are present in at least two setups each, with at least two intentional body poses and the intended delivery orientation represented. |
| Decoupling | At least one reviewed high-impact correlation is broken by an independent group. | Every predeclared high-impact correlation has independent evidence on both sides of a useful contrast. For an expression/wardrobe contrast, e.g., each of the two expressions appears in at least two wardrobe/setting combinations and one wardrobe/setting combination contains both expressions. |

Score 0 where the 1-point condition lacks confirmed evidence. For the decoupling rating, predeclare the relevant pairs and keep a count table, even when inspection shows the archive already meets the 2-point rule. The caps and bounded variety counts prevent redundant views from accumulating extra coverage or diversity credit. Q averages quality across groups instead of rewarding file volume.

**Readiness decision.** Ready for the production-oriented pilot when total ≥75, at least 12 usable training groups, at least two training sessions, level ≥2 for every required scenario, and two adequate independent held-out reference groups for every required scenario. A matching reference is essential for any must-pass facial combination. Pending critical evidence, split leakage, or a missed required scenario blocks readiness. Blank/unknown fields earn no confirmed credit. Log unknowns and either resolve them or use a limited diagnostic run with its purpose recorded, even below 75. A diagnostic run may reveal whether a capture is needed. Keep consent and licensing within the agreed use. The generated-image gate below governs release.

### Score likeness and use

Show reviewers anonymized arm/candidate IDs in randomized order. Provide the genuine held-out reference set with comparable view and expression and at least one independent contextual look. Inspect the face crop at a consistent display size with no beautification or sharpening, and inspect the full image at intended post dimensions (provisionally 1080×1350) and in a normal-size feed preview. Use the original view first so a profile is not penalized for naturally hiding far-side features. When a required face is missing or too small to assess at delivery scale, that image fails its likeness check. Use a calibrated team reviewer for the canonical scores, a second reviewer for borderline or disputed examples, and the creator for separate approval. Keep individual scores in notes if they differ and record the agreed rubric call. Creator veto disqualifies that candidate.

| Signal | Anchors for scoring |
| --- | --- |
| Likeness, crop and post, separately | **0** missing, unjudgeable or clearly a different person. **1** weak family resemblance with several conflicting cues. **2** plausible at a glance but a material trait is off. **3** readily recognizable with only minor drift. **4** strong agreement including view/expression-specific features and recognizable at the judged scale. |
| Prompt adherence | **0** wrong scene/subject class or unusable composition. **1** major requirements missing. **2** a recognizable brief with material misses. **3** meets the brief with minor issues. **4** exact practical execution including the requested fresh attributes. Independently mark whether *every* prespecified mandatory attribute is present, including side, expression or framing when required. |
| Realism | **0** broken. **1** prominent implausibility. **2** visible face, anatomy, texture or light issue at post size. **3** photographic with small issues within agreed use. **4** natural and coherent throughout. |
| Publishability | `yes` satisfies technical/editorial quality at that stage. `editable` can plausibly meet it within approved edits. `no` has a blocker. A final `yes` still requires the separate likeness, prompt, realism and creator approval gates. |

Record drift by feature and direction, e.g. `eyes: farther apart; brows: arch flattened; nose: tip narrower at right 3q; jaw: softened; smile: upper tooth line too straight; asymmetry: reversed`. Add body pose/proportion, outfit bleed, lighting mismatch, cutoffs, copying, and face-detailer changes where applicable. Tag recurring problems with stable failure IDs and document the matched seed, post and arm. Do not infer a person's physical measurements from a generated output.

**Optional embeddings.** Human review is the default. If the applicable terms, permissions and privacy rules support a later local experiment, pin detector, landmark alignment and embedding version. Compare each generated face with multiple independent view-matched references and inspect a consistent statistic such as the median within a scenario. Record coverage, misses, multi-face selection errors and results by yaw, expression, framing, lighting and crop quality. Compare similar and dissimilar creator-rated outputs, and check real–real held-out comparisons under changed views. Check rank agreement and false accepts/rejects at the creator's practical cutoff on calibration data, then validate on a separate batch. Drop or limit the metric when ranking fails within a required condition or only well-lit frontal faces survive detection. A cosine score measures similarity in a verifier's representation and can move with pose, alignment, lighting, face size or expression. The cited studies supply no universal approval threshold. ### Acceptance and what to try next

For each required scenario, apply these editable **heuristics** to the locked production graph. Keep raw rates alongside final rates.

- **Face and prompt reliability.** At least 9 of 12 planned candidate attempts must have *both* final likeness scores ≥3. Separately require at least 9 of 12 with final adherence ≥3 and all mandatory attributes present. Report how often both rules hold on the same candidate.
- **Publishable candidates and posts.** At least 3 of 12 planned attempts must individually meet both previous rules, final realism ≥3, `final_publishability=yes`, creator approval `yes` and the job budget. Require a jointly qualifying approved result in at least 2 of the 3 jobs. The budget is up to four candidates and ten total active editing minutes per job, including unsuccessful candidate edits. Charge time in the logged order. Agree a wall-clock generation ceiling with the team on its hardware if it matters for the posting schedule, and log elapsed generation seconds for every attempt. Keep the editing method and budget consistent across compared arms.
- **Denominators.** Schedule all 12 attempts for the comparison, one candidate per seed. Divide candidate passes by all 12 planned slots. A failed render, missing required face, skipped budget slot or unresolved required rating does not contribute a pass. Keep outcome counts so infrastructure failures can be distinguished from bad images. A retry consumes another scheduled slot under a documented retry rule, or the entire invalid infrastructure run is repeated separately with the original four seeds. Job yield is successful jobs / all three planned jobs, one success per job even if several candidates qualify. Report failed renders, pass counts, image edits attempted and time by arm and scenario. Never pool scenarios to waive a failed requirement. A small grid is an operating decision screen. Repeat the gate on the confirmation grid before calling the workflow ready, and increase the grid across prompts, sessions or training seeds if counts hover around the cutoff.
- **Next action.** If base and LoRA both miss an attribute, test wording, composition controls or graph before changing photos. If early/lower strength obeys the prompt while late/higher strength copies training styling, use the better checkpoint/strength and retest the required scenarios. If the raw face passes and final fails, revise or turn off the detailer and retest. If the same facial defect survives matched prompts/seeds and training checkpoints, and audit evidence is missing or correlated in the affected setup, test a targeted photo intervention. If references disagree, add independent evaluation evidence and repeat. Pause or narrow the publication brief if the candidate cannot clear a required gate under the agreed cost and no tested adjustment yields improvement. Once confirmation passes, stop collecting for that brief unless post needs change.

### Measure the effect of a photo intervention

Start with the smallest change that addresses a specific diagnosis. Before acquiring a photo, check raw/final differences, base control, prompt, model family and load warnings, intended output composition, the actual trainer crops and sidecars. Isolate a caption, preprocessing or inference change in its own arm. If targeted data remains justified, hold fixed base/encoders/VAE, split and references, caption policy, bucket policy, repetitions of shared photos, batch, accumulation, training seed, optimizer, learning rate and schedule shape, timestep/guidance, rank/alpha/target blocks, augmentations, precision, adapter strength and the full evaluation grid. Change only the planned photos plus their captions. Record realized training sizes, LR at checkpoints, updates and approximate exposures. Match checkpoints by logged optimizer step when updates are the comparison. Check estimated versus actual update count using the training logs and checkpoint metadata. | Comparison | What it controls | What changes and how to interpret it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same-size replacement | Same number of files and repeats, nominal per-image exposures and optimizer budget. Match bucket distribution where feasible. | Swapping data tests the value of the new coverage **relative to** the removed captures. Preserve critical groups and track what was removed. |
| Add photos at matched optimizer updates | Closest comparison at equal compute. Use the same starting weights and align the schedule, preferably a fixed schedule with matching warmup. Confirm actual steps because epoch settings can determine step limits in installed trainers. | The old examples get fewer exposures on average. An improvement may reflect new information, reduced dominance of old groups, or both. Report both. |
| Add photos at fixed epochs and repeats | Maintains nominal exposures per image, useful for a system that budgets by epochs or when testing whether existing groups remain equally represented. | More images add optimizer updates and compute and can change scheduler position. An improvement is attributable to combined data and training budget unless a matched-step control separates them. |

For one device with batch and accumulation of 1, one row per training image and uniform repeats, the nominal calculation is `updates = N × repeats × epochs` and exposures per photo are `repeats × epochs` for full epochs. General runs depend on batches per bucket, accumulation, devices and partial epochs. With multiple variants in one group, sum all variant exposures for the group. Earlier/later checkpoints from the same run reveal strength of fit, but comparing checkpoints from runs with different step-dependent schedules may also compare different LR histories.

## Rank the smallest useful follow-up shoot

Create a gap ID from the audit or a recurring pilot failure ID from matched jobs. Link each candidate setup to all of its gaps. Identify the exact visual evidence it would supply, existing photos that could substitute, and the smallest number of independently usable training and reference selects. Choose a setup that can close multiple high-value gaps by changing expression, view and framing within one short session. Separate training from genuinely independent evaluation setups. Do not turn additional frames from the same burst into independent selects.

Score each gap with the following editable **heuristic**. Use the team post calendar to confirm frequency and creator feedback to calibrate severity. The absolute score is a ranking aid, with no measured performance threshold.

| Input | Operational definition |
| --- | --- |
| Importance `I` | 3 required identity/brief gate, 2 materially affects a planned brief, 1 optional creative variation. |
| Frequency `F` | Estimated affected share of approved posts. 3 frequent (at least one-third), 2 recurring (one-tenth to under one-third), 1 occasional (under one-tenth). Use a marked provisional estimate if the calendar is undecided. Conditional uncommissioned ideas can be left out of the rank. |
| Severity `S` | 3 blocks likeness, mandatory control or publication, 2 consumes substantial finishing work or impairs realism, 1 minor. Use 2 provisionally if an untested gap has uncertain severity. A documented missing prerequisite for a required brief can justify a 3. |
| Repeatability `R` | Fraction from 0 to 1 of the relevant matched attempts showing this same reviewed failure. Divide by attempts with enough usable evidence to assess that particular failure and state the denominator in notes. Use 0.5 provisionally for an untested gap. Keep acceptance denominators fixed as described above. Investigate a failure concentrated in one prompt or seed before calling it a data gap. |
| Capture confidence `C` | 0.25 weak/speculative, 0.50 plausible pre-pilot, 0.75 consistent failure, corresponding audit gap and controls checked, 1.00 a matched data change already helped under comparable conditions. When a new capture cannot address the cause, use 0 and route to workflow or training. |
| Effort `E` | 1 quick adjustment in an existing setup, 2 short setup or extra styling, 3 separate location/session or material cost. Score shared setup effort once. |
| Overlap `O` | Fraction from 0 to 1 of this gap's proposed benefit expected from already selected setups. Initialize at 0 and update after each choice. Use observable planned coverage, such as already scheduled right-side, teeth-smile waist-up groups, to estimate it. Avoid double-scoring the same failure under different names. |

For each distinct gap use `I × F × S × R × C × (1 − O)`. Sum the primary and secondary gap terms for a setup, then divide by its effort `E`. Rank highest marginal score first, recalculate remaining overlaps after each selection, and break ties by required status, higher confidence, then lower effort. A required setup closes an outstanding required gate for which capture is the best supported intervention. A conditional setup needs an activated brief or a failed retest demonstrating a distinct gap. An optional setup adds creative range once required gates are on track. Use the computed ranking within those decisions. Stop selecting setups when independent evidence and post-type gates can reasonably be retested, then stop shooting for the current briefs once the confirmatory run passes.

## Template reference

All templates are UTF-8 CSVs with headers only. Keep values, manifests and review media local. Use stable IDs with no personal details, e.g. `IMG-014`, and reuse them across versions. Join exact IDs across files. In a field ending in `_ids` that can hold multiple values, use `|` as the separator and omit spaces. Use CSV quoting for commas and quotes in free text. Blank means not yet evaluated or not applicable, with the distinction explained in notes when relevant. Use `unknown` where it is an explicit enum. A blank/unknown critical input never yields required coverage or a pass. Store scenario definitions, config snapshots, run settings and rating details with versioned local manifests. Fields bearing those IDs point to those manifests.
